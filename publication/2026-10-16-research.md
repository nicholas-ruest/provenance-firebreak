# Provenance Firebreak research — 2026-10-16

## Selected hypothesis

A software-supply-chain incident system can reduce unsafe containment while retaining replayable evidence by composing Sigstore provenance verification, RuVector analogue recall, split-conformal risk control, tier-aware quarantine planning, and RVF witness sealing.

## Candidate comparison

| Candidate | Why considered | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Provenance Firebreak | multi-context incident reconstruction and recovery | selected |
| Attestation Linter | clear provenance boundary | rejected: validator-scale |
| Vector Incident Search | useful recall | rejected: one-feature retrieval service |
| Release Receipt Generator | auditable records | rejected: wrapper without containment or recovery |

## Novelty

JitterMap governs retry admission, ToolRecall ranks tool retrieval, and EvoSeal evolves policy candidates. Provenance Firebreak is materially different: it reconstructs signed-artifact deployment exposure and chooses a calibrated, tier-safe containment plan with cryptographically replayable recovery evidence.

## End-to-end workload

The frozen incident fixture contains a suspect worker artifact, temporal deployment edges, an analogue incident vector, calibration scores, and a tier-0 payments root. The system parses and rejects untrusted provenance, reconstructs exposure, recalls the analogue through a persistent RuVector database, computes the conformal upper-risk gate, plans the smallest safe quarantine, seals its decision with an RVF witness chain, and verifies recovery.

## Limitations

The Sigstore test is a real offline verification-rejection boundary, not a live Fulcio/Rekor production ceremony. Vectors are deterministic domain features. The calibration set and benchmark fixture are small and establish reproducibility rather than external validity.
